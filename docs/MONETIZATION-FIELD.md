# How the field actually charges — evidence, not guesswork

Every figure here was read from a **machine-readable document the vendor
publishes themselves**, on the date shown. Nothing is inferred from a rendered
page, and nothing is estimated. Where a number could not be obtained, the row
says so instead of carrying a plausible guess.

This document exists because `docs/GATEWAYS.md` studies our own tiers in the
abstract, and an abstract tier design is how a product ends up priced by
intuition. The field has already run the experiment.

---

## How this was obtained, since it matters more than the numbers

The competitor survey had been reading rendered HTML with `curl`, which returns
the marketing shell of a client-rendered app and nothing else. Calling that
"browsing their product" overstated it, and the resulting comparison was thinner
than it read.

The route that actually worked, found 2026-08-15:

1. Their `404` JSON body carries a **hint** naming the OpenAPI spec and the API
   reference. Error responses are documentation.
2. `/llms.txt` — a file written *for machines*, describing the product, its
   architecture, its counts and its endpoints in plain text.
3. `/pricing.md` — the pricing page as markdown, ending in a **JSON block** of
   every plan, price and limit.

None of it required a browser, an account or a key. A platform that publishes
for agents has published to us. **We should do the same**, which is now a
concrete item below rather than an observation.

## World Monitor — full tier structure, read 2026-08-15

Source: `https://worldmonitor.app/pricing.md`, self-described as generated by
their own `npm run product:facts`.

| Plan | Monthly | Yearly | Signup | Hard limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | — | **None** | 3 dashboard tabs; 5–15 min refresh |
| **Pro** | $39.99 | $359.99 | Clerk | 10 dashboards, 500 AI req/day, 50 MCP+SDK calls/day |
| **Pro Business** | $49.99 | $449.99 | Clerk | 25 dashboards, 2,500 AI req/day, 250 MCP calls/day |
| **API Starter** | $99.99 | $899.99 | Clerk | 1,000 req/day, 5 webhook rules |
| **API Business** | $299.99 | $2,699.99 | Clerk | 300 req/min, 10,000 req/day, 5 Pro seats |
| **Enterprise** | Custom | — | Contact | SSO/MFA/RBAC, white-label, on-prem, air-gapped |

Annual billing is "3 months free" on Pro tiers and "about 25%" on API tiers.

### What is free, and it is far more than we assumed

**All 56 map layers except one** (Resilience), 500+ feeds over 547+ observed
upstream hosts, country briefs, hotspots, instability scores, chokepoints,
cables, cascade analysis, the breaking-alert pipeline and watchlists — **with no
account at all**.

This is the most important commercial finding in the whole survey. They do not
gate *data*. They gate the **decision layer**: the analyst chat, the scenario
engine, the route explorer, the digest, the custom dashboards, export, and
programmatic access.

### What each paid tier actually sells

- **Pro ($39.99)** — WM Analyst chat across 30+ live services with citations,
  Scenario Engine, Route Explorer, personal AI digest, custom widget builder,
  the Resilience layer, and MCP access to 63 tools under one key. Digest
  delivered to Slack, Discord, Telegram, email or webhook.
- **Pro Business ($49.99)** — Pro plus **data export (CSV, JSON, PDF)** and a
  commercial licence. Note that export is the *only* added capability; the other
  $10 buys the right to use it for client work.
- **API Starter ($99.99)** — REST access, a `wm_` key, structured JSON, cache
  headers, OpenAPI docs, streams and webhooks. Internal use only.
- **API Business ($299.99)** — adds **redistribution rights** and 5 bundled Pro
  seats on the same company email domain.
- **Enterprise** — team workspaces, SSO/MFA/RBAC, white-label and embeddable
  panels, an Android TV app, SIEM connectors, bulk export, AES-256 encrypted
  notification channels, an audit trail and private MCP.

### The licence ladder is the real product

Their tiers are separated less by volume than by **what you are allowed to do
with the output**, stated in plain language:

| Tier | Permission sold |
|---|---|
| Pro | One named person, own research and decisions. Not employer or client work. |
| Pro Business | Same person, but **for company and client work**. Not a customer-facing data product. |
| API Starter | The organisation, in **private** systems. No redistribution. |
| API Business | **Display, embed or deliver outputs inside your own product.** No raw-data resale. |
| Enterprise | Redistribution at volume, white-label, standalone feeds. |

Two details worth copying outright:

- **Attribution is optional.** "Source: World Monitor" is offered, not required.
  A generous stance that costs nothing and buys goodwill.
- **The subscription licence is explicitly separate from the AGPL-3.0 source
  licence**, and from the MIT licence on their thin clients. Three licences,
  each scoped in one sentence. Our own charter's swappability rules need the
  same clarity before we ever take money.

### Overage policy, and it is the honest one

> "Rate limits are hard limits by default: exceeding a plan quota returns HTTP
> `429` with a `Retry-After` header and `X-RateLimit-*` headers. Usage above the
> quota is **rejected — never silently charged**; if opt-in metered overage is
> introduced it will be documented here first."

Rejecting rather than silently billing is a trust position, and it matches the
posture this project takes everywhere else. We already return `429` with
`retryAfterSeconds` and the `X-RateLimit-*` headers; what we lack is the
*written promise* that we will never quietly charge for overage.

## Scale they publish about themselves

Read from `/llms.txt`, same date. These are their claims, labelled as such:

- 500+ curated feeds over **547+ observed upstream hosts**
- **56 map layer types**
- **109 concrete panel implementations**
- **63 MCP tools** under one key
- 2M+ users across 190+ countries; cited as featured in WIRED
- **Six product variants from one codebase** — geopolitical, technology,
  finance, commodities, positive-news and energy — selected at build time by a
  `VITE_VARIANT` environment variable, switchable at runtime from the header
- Stack: TypeScript, Vite, MapLibre GL JS, deck.gl, D3.js, Tauri (desktop);
  Upstash Redis with a three-tier cache (in-memory → Redis → upstream)
- Composite indices with published methodology: **CII v8** instability for 31
  Tier-1 countries, **CRI** resilience across a 196-country rankable universe

### The variant architecture is the strategic idea worth stealing

One codebase, six products, chosen by an environment variable. Our charter
already says "one engine, many gateways" (§2 rule 6) — they have taken the same
principle one step further and shipped it as *separate brands*. Our gateway
registry could do this today; nothing in the architecture forbids it.

## Where this leaves our own pricing

Our current definition (`lib/plans/plans.ts`, published at `/pricing`): Free at
20 investigations/day and 1 monitor; Pro at **10 π / $9** with everything
unlocked. Enforcement is off.

Measured against the field, three things are wrong with it and one is right.

**Right:** free access to the data with no account. That matches the strongest
player in the field exactly, and it is charter §1 anyway.

**Wrong — the gate is in the wrong place.** We gate whole *gateways* (threat,
finance, ownership, procurement are Pro-only). They gate none of their data and
sell the decision layer. Ours is the weaker model: it makes the free product
look thin while giving away the expensive part (the analyst) as soon as someone
pays $9.

**Wrong — one paid tier where the field has five.** There is no commercial
licence, no API tier, no redistribution tier, no enterprise path. A consultant
who wants to use our output for a client has nothing to buy, and the licence
question is simply unanswered.

**Wrong — the price is not anchored to anything.** $9 against their $39.99 is
not a positioning decision, it is an absent one. Either it is deliberate (and
should be stated) or it is a guess (and should be fixed).

### What to do, in order

1. **Move the gate.** Free gets every gateway and every source; paid gets the
   analyst, monitoring at volume, export, and the API. This is a change to
   `lib/plans/plans.ts` and nothing else, because tiers are already data.
2. **Add a commercial licence tier**, and write the permission ladder in one
   sentence per tier the way they do. Without it we cannot be used for client
   work at all, which excludes the entire consulting market.
3. **Add an API tier with a key**, since the API already exists, is already
   documented at `/docs/api`, and is currently unlimited and free forever.
4. **Publish `/pricing.md` and `/llms.txt`.** Both cost one route each. Our
   `lib/api-catalog.ts` and `lib/plans/plans.ts` already hold every fact those
   files need, and the drift test already guards the first one — so unlike
   theirs, ours cannot go stale.
5. **Write the overage promise down.** We already behave correctly; we have
   simply never said so.

## The field splits in two, and the split decides our positioning

The `/llms.txt` route was then tried across thirteen more platforms. Two more
publish one — **SoCRadar** and **Censys** — and both `llms.txt` files point at a
pricing page. Neither page contains a price: both render navigation and a
*Request a Demo* button, with the numbers behind a sales conversation.

That produces the actual market picture:

| Model | Platforms | What a buyer experiences |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-serve, published prices** | World Monitor | Read the price, pay, use it in minutes |
| **Enterprise sales, no published price** | SoCRadar, Censys, Recorded Future, Dataminr, Flashpoint, Silobreaker, Crisis24 | Fill a form, wait for a call, negotiate |

Six of the seven strongest names in the field **will not tell you what they
cost**. The one that publishes everything — price, limits, licence scope, and a
JSON block for machines — is also the one with 2M+ users.

**This is the opening, and it is larger than any feature gap in this document.**
Every capability comparison in `docs/COMPETITORS.md` shows us behind. Pricing
transparency is a place where we can be ahead *today*, at the cost of writing
two files, and where most of the field has structurally chosen not to compete.

Note also that SoCRadar's pricing page was recorded in `docs/UNREACHED.md` as a
`404` — unreachable. It was reachable the whole time; the survey simply had the
wrong URL and never asked the site where its own pricing lived. Their
`llms.txt` answered in one request.

## Still unreached, and honestly so

- **The other 29 platforms' tier structures.** Only this one has been read at
  this depth. The same three-file route (`/llms.txt`, `/pricing.md`, the `404`
  hint) should be tried on every one of them before assuming it will not work.
- **Anything behind a login.** No account was created anywhere, ours included.
  Every number above is a published price, not a purchased one.
- **Their actual usage, revenue or conversion.** The "2M+ users" figure is their
  claim and is recorded as their claim.
