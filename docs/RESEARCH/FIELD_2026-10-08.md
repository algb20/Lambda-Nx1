## 1. One finding that is not optional: EU AI Act Article 50 applies

**Read from the official text:** the Commission's AI Act Service Desk (ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu, articles 50 and 113, read 2026-10-08). EUR-Lex answered an empty 202 to this network, and it was not worked around.

- **Art. 50(4):** "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text which is published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest shall disclose that the text has been artificially generated or manipulated." This does not apply where the content "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control and where a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the publication".
- **Art. 50(5):** the information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner".
- **Art. 113 (as amended):** the Regulation "shall apply from 2 August 2026", with listed exceptions. Article 50 (Chapter IV) is not among them. The high-risk dates moved to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028. Secondary sources name the amending act Regulation (EU) 2026/1744; that is *UNVERIFIED* here.

**Where Lambda stands (REPO-PRESENT, batch 20).** Model-written text appears in two places, the standing brief and the AI analyst panel. Both showed only a vendor and model name ("anthropic · …"), and that does not tell a reader that a model wrote the text. Both now say "AI-generated · provider · model" (`lib/ai/disclosure.ts`, tested). The deterministic reading still says "computed — no model". No AI text reaches email or publishing today. Publishing is paused (R294).

**Owner item (C10):** before publishing resumes (30.27.8.R), the Publication contract must decide one of two things for AI-written text: carry the Art. 50(4) disclosure, or pass a recorded human editorial review. *Lambda's status as "deployer" and whether the brief is "published … to inform the public" are legal questions, and this file does not settle them.*

## 2. Comparable platforms — what moved in 2026

| Platform | What changed (source) | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **World Monitor** (AGPL-3.0, reference only) | v2.10.0, its releases page. New items: a domain-segmented sources catalogue and a `/sources` page; a full provider catalogue; USDA stocks-to-use and USGS/BGS mineral shares; a defence-industry layer; a Country Resilience Index; story corroboration exposed to agents through MCP, and "safe live dashboard controls" via WebMCP; rate limits added to three unguarded public proxies; zh-TW and Swahili. Earlier 2026 releases: server-side feed aggregation (about −95 % edge calls), conditional GET (ETag / If-Modified-Since) on its RSS relay, negative-result caching, and moving 52 endpoints from POST to GET for edge caching. | They now publish a sources page; we already publish credits and licences per finding, which goes further. **DISCOVERED:** conditional GET on feeds would cut our upstream load and courtesy cost; corroboration-over-MCP matches our `app/api/mcp`. |
| **SitDeck** (closed) | Launched 2026-03-06 (Product Hunt). It *claims* 198+ providers, 61+ widgets and 65 map layers, plus an AI analyst chat, a daily briefing and preset dashboards. Its pricing differs between its own site (tiered) and its launch page ("100 % free"). | Unchanged threat: breadth and onboarding speed. Its counts mix providers and layers, the exact thing CLAUDE.md §2a separates. |

| **OpenCTI** (Apache-2.0 core; AI in Enterprise Edition) | v7 (Feb 2026): a browser extension, a 12-month LTS track, and draft-only editing roles. | **Dataminr** (closed) | Completed its acquisition of ThreatConnect (deal announced Oct 2025 at $290 M; closing reported by secondary sources). It pitches "agentic, client-tailored intelligence" that fuses external signals with a client's internal data. | The field is moving to **agents over fused internal and external data**.
